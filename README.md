# Chipotle Mexican Grill (NYSE: CMG) — DCF Valuation Model

A fully formula-driven discounted cash flow valuation of Chipotle Mexican Grill,
generated programmatically with Python and `openpyxl`.

Every historical figure comes from Chipotle's SEC filings, every input cell carries
a source note inside the workbook, and every calculated cell in the Excel output is
a **live formula** — click any number and trace it back to an input.

**Just want to look at the model?** Download
[`output/CMG_DCF_Model.xlsx`](output/CMG_DCF_Model.xlsx). It opens in Excel with all
formulas intact; everything else here is the code that generates it.

| Terminal growth | 2.25% |
| Terminal value as % of EV | 78.2% |
| Sensitivity range (Table A) | $14.13 – $29.71 |

**Reading the gap.** On GDP-anchored terminal assumptions and a CAPM cost of capital,
the DCF does not reproduce the ~20.5x EV/EBITDA the market pays for Chipotle. The
Gordon Growth terminal value implies an exit multiple of 8.6x EBITDA; backing into the
market price instead requires perpetual growth of roughly 5.9%, well above nominal GDP.

So the useful output is a decomposition rather than a price target — the market is
paying for growth and durability beyond what a five-year DCF anchored to GDP can
capture. The reasoning behind each assumption is in
[`docs/methodology.md`](docs/methodology.md).

> Market inputs (share price, 10-year Treasury yield, beta) were retrieved
> **2026-09-06** and do not refresh on their own. To re-run against current data, edit
> `src/wacc.py` and `src/assumptions.py` and rebuild.

## How to run

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate          # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt

python src/build_model.py          # writes output/CMG_DCF_Model.xlsx
open output/CMG_DCF_Model.xlsx     # Windows: start output\CMG_DCF_Model.xlsx
```

To change an assumption, edit `src/assumptions.py` (or `src/wacc.py` for cost of
capital) and re-run. The whole model rebuilds in under a second.

Optional — recalculate every formula and assert the model ties, without opening Excel:

```bash
pip install formulas
python src/verify_model.py
```

Current output: **10/10 checks passed**. It verifies that there are no `#REF!`,
`#DIV/0!`, `#VALUE!` or `#N/A` values anywhere; that the built EBIT ties to EBIT as
reported in every one of the five historical years; that the built restaurant count
ties to the count in the 10-K; that the valuation bridge is internally consistent;
that both sensitivity tables' base-case cells equal the bridge's implied price; and
that terminal growth is below WACC.

## The workbook

Eight tabs, colour-coded by purpose — **blue tabs are inputs**, **orange tabs are
calculations**, **green tabs are outputs**.

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Summary** | One-page conclusion: implied price, upside, EV, key assumptions, and a three-sentence verdict generated by formula from the live model |
| **Historicals** | FY2021A–FY2025A as filed, plus every cost line common-sized as a % of revenue, the working capital build, and restaurant/AUV/comp metrics |
| **Assumptions** | Every forward-looking driver. Revenue is built **restaurant count × AUV**, not a top-line growth rate |
| **WACC** | CAPM cost of equity, capital structure, and an explicit statement of the lease treatment |
| **DCF** | Revenue → EBIT → NOPAT → UFCF → present value, on a mid-year convention |
| **Terminal Value** | Gordon Growth (primary) cross-checked against an exit multiple, with both implied-value checks and automated flags |
| **Valuation Bridge** | EV → equity value → implied share price → upside/downside, plus sanity checks |
| **Sensitivity** | Two live 2-variable tables (WACC × g, WACC × terminal EBIT margin) with a colour scale and a highlighted base case |

### Formatting conventions

- **Blue font** — hardcoded input or historical actual (the only cells you should change)
- **Black font** — formula referencing cells on the same sheet
- **Green font** — formula linking to another sheet
- **No hardcoded numbers inside formulas.** A scan of all 548 formula cells found no
  embedded assumption values — the only numeric literals are the year indices `1`–`5`
  that build the discount period. Every assumption is referenced from its input cell.
- $ in millions to one decimal; percentages to one decimal (WACC and growth to two);
  share prices to two decimals; multiples as `0.0x`
- Freeze panes on the header rows, column widths set, print areas set to
  fit-one-page-wide in landscape

> The print area covers columns A–G (labels and numbers) and deliberately excludes
> column I, which holds the long-form source note for every input. Those notes are
> for on-screen traceability; including them would break the one-page-wide printout.

## Model limitations

**1. Terminal value is 78.2% of enterprise value.** This is normal for a company still
growing units ~7% a year, but it means the answer is a statement about the discount rate
and terminal assumptions far more than about the five forecast years. That makes the
sensitivity tables more informative than the point estimate. Two things partly offset it:
the terminal year is normalised (see below), and both implied cross-checks are shown and
flagged on the tab.

**2. The two terminal methods disagree sharply.** Gordon Growth implies an 8.6x exit
multiple; an 18.0x exit multiple implies 5.9% perpetual growth. Both are flagged
automatically on the Terminal Value tab. I have not "split the difference" — the Gordon
Growth number is the base case because it is the one anchored to fundamentals, and the
disagreement is disclosed rather than averaged away.

**3. Terminal-year capex is normalised, and that is a judgement call.** FY2030E capex runs
at 5.6% of revenue to fund ~370 new restaurants. Perpetuating that alongside 2.25% growth
would be internally inconsistent, so terminal capex is normalised to 3.8% of revenue
(D&A of 3.1% plus ~0.7% of growth capital). Without this the implied price would be
**$2.30 lower ($17.82, or 11% below the base case)**. The unadjusted figure is shown
alongside it on the tab.

**4. Costs are modelled at the EBIT-margin level, not line by line.** The Historicals tab
common-sizes every cost line so the margin assumption is visibly grounded in trend, but the
forecast does not separately project food, labour, occupancy and other operating costs. A
line-by-line build would let you isolate, say, the tariff impact on food costs — but it
would also multiply the number of assumptions without necessarily improving the answer.

**5. Timing gap between the valuation date and the market price.** The model is struck at
fiscal year-end 12/31/2025 (the balance-sheet date of the most recent 10-K) and compared to
a 2026-09-04 market price. Roughly eight months of value accretion is not rolled forward,
which makes the implied downside modestly overstated. See Open Items.

**6. This is a standalone DCF.** There is no comparable-companies or precedent-transactions
analysis. A full valuation would triangulate across all three.

**7. Beta is a single point estimate from one secondary source** rather than regressed from
raw return data or cross-checked against a peer set of unlevered betas.

## Open Items

Things I could not fully verify, and places where a defensible alternative exists.

1. **Equity risk premium.** The model uses **5.0%**, the low end of the 5.0–5.5% mature-market
   range. Damodaran's *implied* ERP for the S&P 500 on 2026-09-01 was **4.14%**. Using 4.14%
   lowers the cost of equity by 81bps to 8.67% and raises the implied price to
   **$22.72 (+12.9%)**. Which figure is "right" is a genuine methodological choice, not an error.
   The 5.0% base case is the more conservative one.

2. **Beta source.** 0.94 is stockanalysis.com's 5-year monthly beta, retrieved 2026-09-06 — a
   secondary source. I do not have Bloomberg or Capital IQ access to cross-check it, and I
   have not regressed it from raw returns. Beta feeds WACC linearly, so a 0.85–1.05 range
   would move WACC by roughly ±50bps.

3. **FY2021 and FY2022 AUV are disclosed to only one decimal** ($2.6m and $2.8m) in the
   filings of those years. That imprecision is why the computed revenue realisation factor
   exceeds 1.0x in FY2021–22. The forecast calibration therefore uses the **FY2023–FY2025**
   average only (0.9867x), where AUV is disclosed to three decimals.

4. **FY2021 diluted share count is derived, not as-filed.** Chipotle reported 28,511k diluted
   shares pre-split; the model shows 1,425,550k = 28,511k × 50 for the June 2024 50-for-1
   split. Chipotle never restated FY2021 in a later filing, so this is my arithmetic. FY2022
   onward are as-restated in the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks. All share figures in the model are
   post-split — no pre-split figure is used anywhere.

5. **Share count has fallen since the valuation date.** The model uses 1,302,423k shares
   outstanding (FY2025 10-K cover, as of 2026-01-30). The Q2 2026 10-Q cover reports
   **1,265,418k** as of 2026-07-24 after further buybacks. Using the current count on the
   current balance sheet would raise the implied price by roughly 3%, but would be
   inconsistent with a 12/31/2025 valuation date and cash balance.

6. **No stub period.** H1 2026 actuals (revenue $6,436.8m, operating income $922.7m) inform
   the FY2026E assumptions but the model does not discount a partial year. A mid-year-2026
   valuation date with a stub would be more precise and materially more complex.

7. **Cash and investments definition.** "Cash and investments" of $1,246.3m at 12/31/2025 =
   cash and equivalents + short-term investments + long-term investments. The long-term line
   includes ~$77m of non-marketable and ~$28m of equity-method investments (the Cultivate Next
   fund), which are not immediately liquid. $35.4m of restricted cash is excluded. Stripping
   the non-marketable investments would reduce the implied price by roughly $0.08.

8. **The 14 partner-operated restaurants are not modelled.** They are licensed, not
   consolidated, and Chipotle does not separately disclose the licensing revenue — it is
   immaterial. Chipotle has **no franchise revenue**; every restaurant in the revenue build is
   company-owned.

9. **Tariffs.** The FY2025 10-K flags an expected tariff impact on food, beverage and packaging
   costs as a forward-looking risk without quantifying it. That pressure is embedded in the
   FY2026E margin trough of 14.5% (set from observed H1 2026 results) but is not separately
   modelled or sized.

10. **The current EV/EBITDA of 20.5x is computed, not sourced.** It uses market cap on the
    model's diluted share count less cash and investments, over FY2025A EBITDA, on a
    leases-as-operating-expense basis. Screeners that capitalise operating leases as debt
    report a materially higher EV and a different multiple — stockanalysis.com shows total debt
    of $5.42bn for CMG, which is the ~$5.08bn of lease liabilities. The two are not comparable;
    mine is consistent with the rest of this model.

11. **FY2026 openings are inferred.** Management guided to 350–370 total restaurants including
    10–15 partner-operated. The model takes the midpoint (360) less ~13 partner-operated = 347
    company-owned. Chipotle did not guide company-owned openings separately.
